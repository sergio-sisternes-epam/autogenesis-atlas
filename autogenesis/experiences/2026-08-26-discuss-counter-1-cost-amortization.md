## Context

Continuing Original Counter 1 under the agent-maintained multiverse refinement. Residual tension was agent cost (context, pruning decisions, navigability).

## What happened

User cost-side argument:

- There is a higher cost in all the inference thrown away every time a session is compacted or discarded.
- Every penny spent today (on structuring the discussion graph) will save inference and human thinking efforts tomorrow.

Framing: the durable, agent-maintained graph is an investment that amortizes. Ephemeral conversation discards the inference work; the graph reifies it so future sessions (and future human thought) do not have to re-derive the same branches, contradictions, and direction changes.

## Outcome

This further weakens the residual cost objection in Counter 1. The comparison is no longer “agent cost of maintaining the graph” versus “zero cost”, but “agent cost of maintaining the graph” versus “repeated waste of inference on discarded sessions + repeated human re-orientation”.

Open: concrete measurement or bounds on the amortization (when does the break-even occur, how is pruning kept cheap enough that the graph does not itself become expensive to query).
