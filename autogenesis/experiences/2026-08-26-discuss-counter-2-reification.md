## What happened

**Original Counter 2**  
Graphs readily encode false, vague, or inconsistent knowledge—especially when content is provisional.

Knowledge graphs suffer from false assertions, subjective/vague predicates, internal contradictions, hierarchy explosion, uncontrolled property growth, and concept–instance confusion. These problems appear in both hand-curated and auto-generated graphs. Representational formalisms are optimised for declarative/assertional knowledge and struggle with modalities (belief, rejection, undecided), uncertainty, and genuine disagreement.

Because discussion content is explicitly “ideas that are forming. Not absolute,” turning those ideas into first-class nodes and typed edges risks reifying provisional claims. Later contraction becomes politically and mechanically costly once other pages or other Atlases have related_to links pointing at them.

Under the agent-maintained multiverse model already adopted for Counter 1, this risk is partially mitigated (contradictions can co-exist as alternative realities), but the core reification problem remains: once an idea is a durable node with edges, it acquires inertia.
