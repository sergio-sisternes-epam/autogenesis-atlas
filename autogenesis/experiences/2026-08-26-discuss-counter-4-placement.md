## What happened

**Original Counter 4**  
Placement, cross-Atlas exploration, and ontological agreement create coordination and scope-creep costs.

There is rarely a single “best place” or globally agreed modelling of a domain. Different stakeholders hold different semantics and even contradictory claims. Semantic-Web-style critiques highlight the brittleness of requiring ontological agreement. Adding a rule that “if the subject has an Atlas, locate the best place” plus “explore any relevant atlas” introduces navigation and decision overhead every time a discussion starts or a new node appears.

In a multi-skill, multi-Atlas environment this can turn a quick exploratory exchange into a search-and-placement ritual.

Under the agent-maintained model already adopted, the agent (not the human) would pay this overhead. The counter still asks whether that overhead is justified for every discussion, and whether free cross-Atlas exploration expands scope faster than the TVA evaluation can contain it.
